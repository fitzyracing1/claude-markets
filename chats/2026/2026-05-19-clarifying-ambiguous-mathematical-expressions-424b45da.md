**If it's gradual (years away)**
Traditional financial security mostly collapses in relevance. What holds value shifts toward:
- **Hard assets** — land, food-producing property, water access
- **Commodities** — fuel, medicine, seeds, tools

**If it's sudden (months away)**

- Stockpiling physical necessities
- Community relationships and mutual aid networks
- Geographic relocation (bunkers, remote land)

**The honest truth about "financial security"**
In a true end-of-world scenario, **money itself becomes the least useful thing**. Every major civilization collapse in history shows the same pattern — social capital (trust, skills, relationships) outlasts financial capital every time.

**What billionaires actually do** (seriously)
Many ultra-wealthy people hedge against collapse with remote properties in New Zealand, private islands, food stores, and generators — essentially abandoning financial instruments entirely.

Great question — this is actually a fascinating monetary policy idea. Let's break it down:

**How it would theoretically work**
Instead of the dollar being backed by nothing (fiat) or gold, every dollar in circulation would be tied to the value of a basket of stocks — essentially the S&P 500 or a broader market index.

**Potential benefits**
- The dollar would be tied to **real productive output** — actual companies making real things
- It could **curb inflation** since money supply would be anchored to something tangible
- More democratic than gold — stock value reflects the whole economy, not one commodity
- Could incentivize **long-term economic growth** since a stronger economy = stronger dollar

**The serious problems**

- **Volatility** — the stock market drops 30-40% in crashes. Your dollar's value would do the same overnight
- **Circular logic** — stocks are priced *in dollars*, so you'd have a self-referencing loop that could spiral
- **Who controls the index?** Deciding which stocks back the dollar becomes enormous political power
- **Market manipulation** — bad actors could attack the currency by crashing specific stocks
- **Recessions get worse** — economic downturns would simultaneously crash stocks *and* the dollar at the same time, doubling the pain

**The deeper issue**
The dollar's real backing today is **US government credibility and military/economic power** — essentially a trust system. Tying it to markets trades one form of faith for another, but adds volatility.
